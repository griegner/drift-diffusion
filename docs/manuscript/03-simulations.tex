\section{Validation by Simulation}\label{sec:simulations}
\subsection{Simulation Settings}\label{sec:simulation-settings}
The finite-sample performance of the pseudo-MLE $\hat{\bm\beta}^*$ \eqref{eq:beta_}, equivalently $\hat{\bm\theta}^*$ without covariates,  and the covariance estimators $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$--$\widehat{V}_\text{SH}$ \eqref{eq:V_ar}--\eqref{eq:V_sh} was assessed via Monte Carlo simulations across three settings. In each setting, $b =$ 900 independent replications of a sequence of choice and reaction times $\bm{Y} = (Y_1, \dots, Y_n)$ of length $n =$ 1000 were simulated under the true density ($f_0$; \ref{eq:f_dep}) and fit under the working density ($f^*$; \ref{eq:f_ind}). Settings vary along two dimensions: specification ($f_0 = f^*$ or $f_0 \neq f^*$) and parameter structure (fixed or varying across trials), which together determine whether the information matrix equality $\mathcal{H}_{\bm\beta} = \mathscr{I}_{\bm\beta}$ holds and which covariance estimators are consistent (Section~\ref{sec:covariance-estimation}). The first two settings are correctly specified; the second additionally demonstrates modeling of nonstationarity through covariates. The third tests robustness to unmodeled parameter variability and temporal dependence.

\begin{itemize}

    \item \emph{Correct specification, constant parameters} ($f_0 = f^*$): a baseline correctly specified setting with parameters $a,t_0,|v|,z$ fixed across trials. Stimulus direction determines the drift sign. Decision outcomes are $iid$, the information matrix equality holds, and all four covariance estimators are expected to be consistent (Figure 2).

    \item \emph{Correct specification, trending decision boundary} ($f_0 = f^*$): a correctly specified setting where the decision boundary increases linearly from $0.8\beta_a$ to $1.2\beta_a$ over the session, with midpoint value $\beta_a=0.625$, while the parameters $t_0,|v|,z$ remain constant. The working model fits $a_i=\beta_a x_i$. The information matrix equality holds, and all four estimators are expected to be consistent. This setting demonstrates modeling DDM parameters as functions of covariates (Section~\ref{sec:covariates}) and explicitly accounting for nonstationary parameter trends with the trial number covariate (Figure 3).

    \item \emph{Misspecification and temporal dependence} ($f_0 \neq f^*$): a misspecified setting where $a$ is fixed and $t_0,|v|,z$ switch jointly under one stationary two-state Markov chain. The working model includes stimulus direction but omits the state, estimating four constant parameters $a,t_0,|v|,z$. This constitutes misspecification of types (i) and (iii) (Section~\ref{sec:misspecification}). Only $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$ accounts for both marginal misspecification and serial dependence, while $\widehat{V}_\text{MR}$ addresses only the former (Figure 4).

\end{itemize}

Parameter centers were taken from \citet{matzke_psychological_2009} (Table~3, means), rescaled to the present implementation: $\bar{a} = 0.625$, $\bar{t}_0 = 0.435$, $\overline{|v|} = 2.23$, $\bar{z} = 0.008$. These are the true parameters in the constant setting, with $\bar{a}$ serving as the midpoint $\beta_a$ in the trending boundary setting.

In the third setting, $a=0.625$, while $(t_0,|v|,z)$ switches between engaged $(0.39538,3.56,0.008)$ and disengaged $(0.47462,0.90,0.34979)$ states. The engaged state combines a shorter non-decision delay, stronger evidence accumulation, and a nearly neutral starting point. The disengaged state has a longer delay, weaker accumulation, and rightward bias. Measured indicators of engagement, such as features extracted from behavioral videos, could help model these persistent unmodeled decision states. However, such indicators are often unavailable or omitted from the fitted model as this example demonstrates.

State differences were based on the reported across-trial variability in drift rate, non-decision time, and starting point after rescaling to our parameterization \citep{matzke_psychological_2009}. The chain starts with equal probabilities for the two states and remains in its current state with probability $0.95$, giving a mean state duration of 20 trials per state \citep{ashwood_mice_2022}. Each replication in this setting draws new states and stimuli, and in all three settings, stimulus directions are independently randomized with equal probabilities and determine the drift sign in both the generating and working models.

Pseudo-true parameters (Section~\ref{sec:misspecification}), which need not equal generating means, were approximated by fitting the working model to an independent 50,000-trial sequence from the third setting. Fits used the model's default initialization, with all four parameters freely estimated except that the coefficient $\beta_a$ replaces $a$ in the second setting. We compared mean analytic standard errors and correlations with their empirical counterparts across replications.

\subsection{Simulation Results}

\subsubsection{Correct specification, constant parameters}
Under correct specification and independent trials (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_constant}), robust covariance corrections provide no consistent advantage. Mean parameter estimates are within $0.09$ empirical standard deviations of their true values, and mean SH and OP standard errors are within $4\%$ of the empirical values.

\subsubsection{Correct specification, trending decision boundary}
Including the trial number covariate accounts for the increasing boundary $a_i$ over the session (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_linear}). Mean estimates of the coefficient $\beta_a$ and the parameters $t_0,|v|,z$ are within $0.10$ empirical standard deviations of their true values, and mean SH standard errors are within $2\%$ of the empirical values. Across all four covariance estimators, the largest absolute SE bias is $5.2\%$. These results show that explicitly modeling the boundary trend allows standard errors to remain accurate across all covariance estimators.

\subsubsection{Misspecification and temporal dependence}
With persistent unobserved states (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_realistic}), the constant parameters describe a working-model approximation to a mixture of decision states. Correcting marginal misspecification alone is insufficient: MR underestimates the standard error of $|v|$ by $53\%$, compared with $10\%$ for AR. Temporal dependence introduces by this setting also .... AR therefore recovers much of the uncertainty missed by treating trials as independent. The remaining $2.4$--$9.5\%$ underestimation across parameters is consistent with finite-sample bias ....

Known parameter trends can be modeled through covariates, while persistent omitted variation requires a temporal-dependence correction. The correction improves uncertainty estimates for the working model's parameters but does not recover the omitted state-specific parameters.
