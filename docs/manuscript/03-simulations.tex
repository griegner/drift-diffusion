\section{Validation by Simulation}\label{sec:simulations}
\subsection{Simulation Settings}\label{sec:simulation-settings}
The finite-sample performance of the pseudo-MLE $\hat{\bm\beta}^*$ \eqref{eq:beta_}, equivalently $\hat{\bm\theta}^*$ without covariates,  and the covariance estimators $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$--$\widehat{V}_\text{SH}$ \eqref{eq:V_ar}--\eqref{eq:V_sh} was assessed via Monte Carlo simulations across three settings. In each setting, $b =$ 900 independent replications of a sequence of choice and reaction times $\bm{Y} = (Y_1, \dots, Y_n)$ of length $n =$ 1000 were simulated under the true density ($f_0$; \ref{eq:f_dep}) and fit under the working density ($f^*$; \ref{eq:f_ind}). Settings vary along two dimensions: specification ($f_0 = f^*$ or $f_0 \neq f^*$) and parameter structure (fixed or varying across trials), which together determine whether the information matrix equality $\mathcal{H}_{\bm\beta} = \mathscr{I}_{\bm\beta}$ holds and which covariance estimators are consistent (Section~\ref{sec:covariance-estimation}). The first two settings are correctly specified, the second additionally demonstrates modeling of nonstationarity through covariates, while the third tests robustness to unmodeled parameter variability and temporal dependence.

\begin{itemize}

    \item \emph{Correct specification, constant parameters} ($f_0 = f^*$): a baseline correctly specified setting with parameters fixed across trials ($\bm{\theta}_i = \bm{\theta}^*$, no covariates). Decision outcomes are $iid$, the information matrix equality holds, and all four covariance estimators are expected to be consistent.

    \item \emph{Correct specification, trending decision boundary} ($f_0 = f^*$): a correctly specified setting where the decision boundary increases linearly from $0.8\beta_a$ to $1.2\beta_a$ over the session, with midpoint value $\beta_a = 0.63$, while all other parameters are held constant. The information matrix equality holds, and all four estimators are expected to be consistent. This setting demonstrates two capabilities of the pseudo-MLE: modeling DDM parameters as functions of covariates (Section~\ref{sec:covariates}), and explicitly accounting for nonstationary parameter trends with measured covariates.

    \item \emph{Misspecification and temporal dependence} ($f_0 \neq f^*$): a misspecified setting where $a$ is fixed and $t_0,|v|,z$ switch jointly under one stationary two-state Markov chain. The working model includes stimulus direction but omits the state, estimating four constant coefficients $a,t_0,|v|,z$. This constitutes misspecification of types (i) and (iii) (Section~\ref{sec:misspecification}). Only $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$ accounts for both marginal misspecification and serial dependence. $\widehat{V}_\text{MR}$ addresses only the former.

\end{itemize}

Parameter centers were taken from \citet{matzke_psychological_2009} (Table~3, means), rescaled to the present implementation: $\bar{a} = 0.63$ (rounded from $0.625$), $\bar{t}_0 = 0.435$, $\bar{v} = 2.23$, $\bar{z} = 0.008$. These are the true parameters in the constant setting, with $\bar{a}$ serving as the midpoint $\beta_a$ in the trending boundary setting.

In the third setting, $a=0.63$, while $(t_0,|v|,z)$ switches between engaged $(0.38217,3.56,0.008)$ and disengaged $(0.48783,0.90,0.34979)$ states, with $t_0$ in seconds. The engaged state combines a shorter nondecision delay, stronger evidence accumulation, and a nearly neutral starting point. The disengaged state has a longer delay, weaker accumulation, and rightward bias. Measured indicators of engagement, such as pupil diameter or features extracted from behavioral videos, could help model these persistent latent unmodeled decision states. Such indicators are often unavailable or omitted from the fitted model.

State contrasts were derived from the reported mean variability parameters: drift SD $\eta=0.133$ and uniform widths $s_t=0.183$, $s_z=0.037$ \citep{matzke_psychological_2009, vandekerckhove_diffusion_2008}. Converting from noise SD $0.1$ and boundary separation $A=0.125$ gives $\sigma_v=\eta/0.1=1.33$, $\sigma_{t_0}=s_t/\sqrt{12}=0.05283$, and $\sigma_z=(2s_z/A)/\sqrt{12}=0.17090$. For engaged and disengaged states, respectively, we set $t_0=\bar{t}_0\mp\sigma_{t_0}$, $|v|=\bar{v}\pm\sigma_v$, $z_E=\bar{z}$, and $z_D=\bar{z}+2\sigma_z$. Equal state probabilities preserve these marginal variances, while the pooled $z$ mean shifts to $0.17890$. The original bias ratios, $0.504$ and $0.675$, remain within the reported range $0.272$--$0.782$.

For state $S_i\in\{E,D\}$, signed drift is $v_i=x_i|v|(S_i)$, where stimulus directions $x_i\in\{-1,+1\}$ are equiprobable and independent of each other and the chain; positive $z$ always favors rightward responses. The chain starts at stationarity with equal state probabilities and remains in its current state with probability $0.95$ (mean duration 20 trials and lag-one correlation $0.90$). Each replication draws fresh states and stimuli, with outcomes independent conditional on both.

Pseudo-true coefficients, which need not equal generating means, were computed independently of the Monte Carlo samples by integrating the expected working log-likelihood over both states and stimulus directions. Fits in this setting used the model's default initialization, with all four coefficients freely estimated. All 900 fits were retained, including five with trust-region warnings whose Newton refinements changed parameters by less than $5\times10^{-7}$ empirical standard errors. We compared analytic standard errors and correlations with their empirical counterparts across replications.

\subsection{Simulation Results}

\subsubsection{Correct specification, constant parameters}
Simulations with constant parameters (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_constant}A,B) serve as a baseline for correct specification ($f_0 = f^*$). Point estimates (A; diagonals) are approximately unbiased, with sampling distributions centered near the true parameters. Pairwise correlations (A; off-diagonals) are positive for $(a, v)$ and $(t_0, z)$, negative for $(a, t_0)$, $(t_0, v)$, and $(v, z)$, and near zero for $(a, z)$. Analytic standard error estimates (B; diagonals) are generally close to their empirical counterparts. Relative biases for the sample-Hessian estimator are within 2.1\%, whereas the outer-product estimator overestimates standard errors for $t_0$ (9.7\%) and $z$ (5.4\%). The robust estimators underestimate standard errors by 1.2--4.0\%. Sample-Hessian estimates have the lowest sampling variance across all four parameters. Analytic pairwise correlation estimates (B; off-diagonals) are generally close to the empirical correlations, with modest estimator-specific biases.

\subsubsection{Correct specification, trending decision boundary}
Simulations where the decision boundary increases linearly over the session (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_linear}A,B) are also correctly specified ($f_0 = f^*$) because nonstationarity is explicitly modeled by the time covariate. Results are mostly comparable to the constant parameter simulation above (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_constant}). Point estimates (A; diagonals) remain approximately unbiased, now replacing the parameter $a$ with the midpoint value $\beta_a$. Pairwise correlations (A; off-diagonals) remain qualitatively similar. Analytic standard error estimates (B; diagonals) are mostly accurate: relative biases for $\widehat{V}_\text{SH}$ are within 3\%, while $\widehat{V}_\text{OP}$ shows an upward bias most notably for $t_0$ (11\%), and the robust estimators $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$ and $\widehat{V}_\text{MR}$ show slight downward biases for $t_0$ and $z$ (3--4\%). Across parameters and covariance estimators, empirical coverage of nominal 95\% confidence intervals ranges from 92.7\% to 96.6\%. Analytic pairwise correlation estimates (B; off-diagonals) are generally close to the empirical correlations, with modest downward biases for $(\beta_a,t_0)$, $(\beta_a,z)$, and $(t_0,v)$, and for $(t_0,z)$ under the robust estimators. These results suggest that the covariance estimators remain mostly accurate when nonstationary trends are explicitly modeled.

\subsubsection{Misspecification and temporal dependence}
Switching between persistent latent unmodeled decision states (Figure~\ref{fig:sim_realistic}A,B) produces both marginal misspecification and serial dependence. Point estimates (A; diagonals) concentrate near the pseudo-true coefficients $(a^*,t_0^*,|v|^*,z^*) \approx (0.617,0.382,1.476,0.052)$. Analytic standard errors (B; diagonals) show that correcting marginal misspecification alone is insufficient: $\widehat{V}_\text{MR}$ underestimates standard errors for $|v|$ by 54\%, compared with 8\% for $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$ and 56--57\% for the non-robust estimators. AR also recovers the negative correlation between $a$ and $|v|$ estimates (B; off-diagonals): the empirical correlation is $-0.56$, while estimates average $+0.29$, $+0.43$, $+0.13$, and $-0.54$ for $\widehat{V}_\text{SH}$, $\widehat{V}_\text{OP}$, $\widehat{V}_\text{MR}$, and $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$, respectively. Across coefficients, $\widehat{V}_\text{AR}$ underestimates standard errors by 1.9--8.7\%.

Overall, point estimates recovered the true or pseudo-true parameters, while autocorrelation-robust covariances improved uncertainty estimates under unmodeled variability and temporal dependence.
