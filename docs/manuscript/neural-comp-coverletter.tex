On behalf of my co-authors Armin Schwartzman and Pamela Reinagel, I am pleased to submit our manuscript, ``Quantifying uncertainty in drift diffusion models of decision making under temporal dependence and parameter variability,'' for your consideration.

In this paper, we formalize and evaluate a computationally efficient pseudo-maximum-likelihood approach for estimating drift diffusion model (DDM) parameters and their analytical uncertainties in settings with temporal dependence, nonstationarity, and unmodeled parameter variability. Current methods for estimating DDM parameters often either do not provide uncertainty quantification or rely on restrictive assumptions such as independent trials and time-invariant parameters, limiting valid statistical inference and hypothesis testing. Our approach addresses this limitation by providing formal uncertainty estimates under weaker and more realistic assumptions, while also allowing nonstationary variability to be modeled through observed covariates.

We demonstrate these advances through theoretical analysis, simulations, and an application to rat perceptual decision-making data, showing that the method yields accurate inference and reveals dynamic decision-making states across multiple timescales. More broadly, this work provides a methodological foundation for studying temporal variability in decision making, which we believe to be of considerable interest to the field.

To support reproducibility, we provide a Python implementation of the methods on GitHub (\href{https://github.com/griegner/drift-diffusion}{https://github.com/griegner/drift-diffusion}), as well as a Code Ocean capsule containing the data, scripts, and computational environment needed to reproduce the reported results\\ (\href{https://doi.org/10.24433/CO.5868634.v1}{https://doi.org/10.24433/CO.5868634.v1}). Note that the Code Ocean DOI is pending verification and should be active in the next few days.

None of the material in this manuscript has been published or is under consideration for publication elsewhere, other than a \emph{Society for Neuroscience 2026} abstract and \emph{bioRxiv} preprint. All authors have been involved in the work, have approved the manuscript, and agree to its submission.
